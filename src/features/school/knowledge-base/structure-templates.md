# Structure Templates

Shape guides for each document type the Study Generator can produce. These
are skeletons, not fill-in-the-blank forms — adapt section count and depth
to the actual material, don't pad to match the template.

## Study guide + practice questions

1. **Overview** (2-4 sentences) — what this material covers and why it
   matters in the course, not a restatement of the title.
2. **Key concepts** — one subsection per major concept, each with:
   - A plain-language definition or explanation
   - A concrete example (not a repeat of the definition in other words)
   - How it connects to the concept before/after it, if relevant
3. **Common mix-ups** — 2-4 things students commonly confuse in this
   material specifically (e.g., "mitosis vs. meiosis," "affect vs.
   effect"), only if genuine confusions exist for this topic — don't
   invent generic ones to fill space.
4. **Practice questions** — mixed difficulty, answer key separate from
   the questions (so it can double as a real quiz). Include the
   reasoning for at least the harder answers, not just the answer.

## Essay analysis

1. **Thesis identification** — state the work's central argument/theme in
   one or two sentences, in the writer's own terms, not generic
   plot summary.
2. **Structural walkthrough** — how the argument or narrative builds,
   section by section or act by act. Note *why* the structure was chosen
   (build tension, establish contrast, etc.), not just *what* happens.
3. **Evidence and technique** — specific devices, quotes, or rhetorical
   moves, each tied to what they accomplish for the larger argument.
4. **Open questions** — for higher grade bands, 1-3 genuine interpretive
   questions the essay/text leaves open. Skip this section for
   elementary-level output.

## Scene-by-scene / chapter-by-chapter breakdown

For each scene/chapter:
- **What happens** — plot facts, kept tight (this isn't the deliverable,
  it's scaffolding for the analysis below).
- **Why it matters** — what this scene does for character, plot, or
  theme that couldn't be cut without losing something.
- **One notable detail** — an image, line, or choice worth remembering,
  with a one-sentence note on why it's worth remembering.

Don't summarize every scene at equal length — a pivotal chapter deserves
more space than a transitional one.

## Worksheet

1. A short instructional block (2-4 sentences) framing what skill this
   worksheet practices.
2. Problems/prompts grouped by sub-skill, easiest first.
3. An answer key as a separate, clearly-labeled section — never
   interleaved with the questions, so the sheet can be handed to a
   student as-is.
