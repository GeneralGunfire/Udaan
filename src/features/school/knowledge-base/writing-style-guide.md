# Writing Style Guide: Natural, Human-Sounding Prose

Applies to every document the Study Generator produces, regardless of
grade level or document type. Grade level changes vocabulary and sentence
complexity (see `tone-guides.md`); this file changes texture — how the
writing avoids reading like generic AI output.

## Vary sentence length on purpose

Strings of same-length, same-shape sentences are the single biggest
tell of generated text. Mix it up:

- A short sentence lands harder after two longer ones.
- Not every sentence needs a subordinate clause. Some should just be a
  statement.
- If three sentences in a row start with "This" or "The," rewrite one.

## Cut the transitional scaffolding

Ban or heavily ration these — they're the connective tissue that makes
text feel machine-assembled rather than written:

- "Furthermore," "Moreover," "Additionally," "In conclusion,"
  "It's important to note that," "It's worth noting that"
- "This highlights/demonstrates/underscores/showcases the importance of..."
- "Let's dive into..." / "Let's explore..." / "In today's world..."
- Starting a summary paragraph with "In summary" or "Overall" — just
  write the summary; the reader can tell it's a summary from where it sits.

If a sentence would fall apart without its transition word, the logic
wasn't doing enough work — fix the logic, not the transition.

## Concrete over generic

- "The chemical reaction releases energy as heat, visible as the flask
  warming within seconds" beats "This process releases a significant
  amount of energy."
- Use the specific number, name, or example the source material gives
  before reaching for a vague category word.
- When explaining a concept, one well-chosen concrete example beats three
  abstract restatements of the same idea.

## Let claims be plain

- Don't hedge everything with "may," "could potentially," "in some
  cases" when the material actually supports a direct statement.
  Reserve hedges for genuine uncertainty.
- Don't oversell with "incredibly," "profoundly," "a testament to" —
  these inflate small claims and, ironically, make writing sound less
  confident, not more.

## Structure carries weight so prose doesn't have to

- Headers, bullets, and bold key terms exist so the prose itself can stay
  plain and direct — it doesn't need to announce its own organization
  ("First, we will discuss... Next, we will cover..."). Let the heading
  do that job.
- A bullet list item is a phrase or sentence, not a mini-essay. If an
  item needs three sentences, it's probably a paragraph that escaped.

## Read-aloud test

Before finalizing, silently read a paragraph as if speaking it. If a
sentence has no natural place to breathe, or a phrase no one would
actually say out loud ("it is imperative to recognize that"), rewrite it.
This one check catches most of what remains after the rules above.
